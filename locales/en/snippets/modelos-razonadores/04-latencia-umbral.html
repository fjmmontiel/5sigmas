Latency, cost and product design for reasoning models
Once test-time compute becomes real seconds of waiting, theoretical tradeoffs become interaction and economics decisions.
< 0.1 sInstantaneous

The system feels directly responsive. No special waiting-state design is needed.

0.1–1 sFluid

The pause is noticeable, but the user normally keeps their train of thought.

1–10 sNeeds feedback

Progress or an active loading state becomes necessary. Around 5–7 seconds users begin to disengage.

> 10 sReasoning territory

Many hard reasoning requests operate here. Without explicit UX, the default experience feels broken.

Illustrative end-to-end latency · LLM + tools + RAG

A medium-complexity agentic request can spend time in several places, not only model reasoning.

Reasoning · 7–8 sTools/APIs · 6–7 sRAG · 4–5 s
Implication: optimize both the model budget and the surrounding system—retrieval caches, parallel tool calls and faster dependencies.

Reasoning makes cost variable per request. A longer internal chain can cost more even when the visible answer is short.

Reasoning tokens are billable compute

When providers charge reasoning/thinking as output or inference compute, deeper chains increase the bill whether or not users see those tokens.
