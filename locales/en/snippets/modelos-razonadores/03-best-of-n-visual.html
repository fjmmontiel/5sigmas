Test-time compute · sampling

Best-of-N: generate several answers, then select

The simplest way to spend more inference compute is to sample multiple independent candidates. The selector determines what “best” means.

Same prompt, N independent runs at temperature > 0, then a selection rule.

PromptProve that the angles of a triangle sum to 180°.
Candidate 1

Construct a line through A parallel to BC; alternate interior angles map α and β onto a straight angle.

0.91 · selected
Candidate 2

Use the exterior-angle sum and the supplementary relationship at each vertex.

0.78
Candidate 3

Apply the general polygon formula (n − 2) × 180° for n=3.

0.65
PRM selectorCandidate 1 wins because its intermediate steps receive the highest average process score.
Verifier · N≈4–64

Best-of-N + PRM/ORM

Score every candidate and take the maximum.

Best fit: mathematics and code with objective checks.
Vote · N≈8–40

Self-consistency

Select the final answer that appears most frequently across samples.

Best fit: discrete answers and single-result calculations.
Judge · N≈3–8

Model-as-judge

A separate evaluator ranks open-ended candidates against explicit criteria.

Best fit: analysis, writing and arguments without a deterministic verifier.

The expected gain is strongly diminishing: early samples buy much more than later ones.
