Electricity and human development: the curve is steepest at low access

Reliable electricity creates the largest incremental gains when it unlocks basic services for the first time. At higher consumption, many other social and institutional factors increasingly explain differences in outcomes.

Low access

An additional unit of reliable electricity can unlock lighting, refrigeration, communications or pumping that was previously impossible.

Established access

Reliability, affordability and distribution matter as much as the national average number of kWh.

High use

Electricity remains enabling infrastructure, but institutions, education, health systems and income distribution explain more of the remaining welfare differences.

Necessary does not mean sufficient.High human development requires substantial infrastructure, but high electricity consumption by itself does not guarantee that the value reaches everyone.
