Efficiency can reduce energy per task while increasing total demand

This is the rebound effect: lower cost per unit of useful work can make the technology attractive in many more places.

100 requests×10 energy units/request=1,000 units

High cost limits how many products and users can justify the workload.

1,000 requests×2 energy units/request=2,000 units

The task is five times more efficient, but usage grows tenfold, so total demand doubles.

Demand falls

Efficiency improves faster than total usage grows.

Demand stays flat

Usage growth roughly offsets the efficiency gain.

Demand rises

Lower cost unlocks enough new usage to dominate the efficiency improvement.

This is why energy per token, query or image is not enough to forecast system-level demand. You also need adoption and workload growth.
