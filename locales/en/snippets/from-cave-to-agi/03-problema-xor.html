XOR · LINEAR SEPARABILITY

Why one perceptron cannot learn XOR

A perceptron can only draw one straight decision boundary. XOR arranges its two classes on alternating corners, so no single line can separate them.

AND · separable ✓
0001
Three zeros cluster together; one line can isolate the 1.
XOR · not separable ✗
0110
The classes alternate diagonally; every line misclassifies at least one point.
What the perceptron computes
w₁A + w₂B + b

If the result is above zero, predict class 1; otherwise class 0. Changing the weights moves or rotates the boundary, but it remains a line.

The algebraic contradiction
(0,0)→0: b<0(0,1)→1: w₂+b>0(1,0)→1: w₁+b>0(1,1)→0: w₁+w₂+b<0

Adding the two positive-output inequalities implies w₁+w₂>−2b>−b, while the final condition requires w₁+w₂<−b. No weights satisfy all four constraints.

Why hidden layers solve it

Two hidden units can construct separate boundaries—roughly OR and AND—and remap the four inputs into a representation where the XOR-positive cases become linearly separable. The hidden layer changes the geometry of the problem.
