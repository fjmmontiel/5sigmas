SCALING LAWS

Scaling became predictable enough to plan

Empirical scaling laws showed that average loss often follows smooth power-law trends as model size, data and compute increase. Resource allocation became a measurable design problem rather than pure trial and error.

The core relationship
L(x) ≈ A · x−α + B

The exponent depends on what is scaled and on the experimental setup. These are empirical regularities, not universal physical laws.

What scaling laws can tell you
  • How average validation loss changes with resource scale.
  • Whether a model is undertrained relative to its data budget.
  • How to trade parameters, tokens and compute under a fixed training budget.
What they do not tell you

A smooth loss curve does not specify exactly which downstream capability will improve, when a benchmark threshold will be crossed, or whether a new system remains safe and useful. Predictable average loss is not the same as predictable behavior on every task.
