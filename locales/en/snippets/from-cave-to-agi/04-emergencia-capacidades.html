EMERGENT ABILITIES?

Real threshold or measurement artifact?

Some benchmarks appear to jump suddenly as models scale. Whether the underlying capability itself is discontinuous—or the metric turns gradual improvement into a step—is an open empirical question.

Illustrative task

Character-level questions can look like a binary transition if the score is simply “fully correct / incorrect,” even while partial performance improves continuously.

Strong reading · genuine emergence

With a binary metric, several smaller models can score near zero before a larger model crosses the threshold. This motivates the claim that qualitatively new abilities may appear abruptly.

Cautious reading · metric artifact

With partial credit or a smoother metric, the same underlying progression may look continuous. Some apparent “emergence” can therefore be created by the evaluation function.

Why the distinction matters

If important capabilities truly appear discontinuously, extrapolating from smaller systems becomes harder. If apparent thresholds mainly come from metrics, better continuous evaluations can reveal progress earlier. Safety and forecasting depend on knowing which case applies.
