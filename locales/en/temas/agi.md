# AGI: Artificial General Intelligence

**Artificial General Intelligence (AGI)** generally refers to an AI system that can perform a broad range of cognitive tasks at a level comparable to, or above, human capability without task-specific retraining for every new problem. This is a proposed capability, not a universally agreed certification level. There is no single accepted test for AGI, and publicly reported model performance on individual benchmarks does not establish that a system has achieved it.

Different definitions emphasize different criteria. Some focus on performance across economically valuable tasks; others require robust reasoning, planning, autonomous learning and transfer between unfamiliar domains. A useful distinction is between **Artificial Narrow Intelligence (ANI)**, which is optimized for a narrower task or setting, and AGI, which would generalize reliably across many settings.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does AGI exist today?**
No publicly demonstrated system has conclusively satisfied a definition of AGI accepted across the field. Language models can perform impressively on language, mathematics, code and other benchmarks, but strong scores on selected tasks do not prove broad, reliable transfer, long-horizon planning or autonomous learning in unfamiliar situations.

**When will AGI arrive?**
There is no consensus. Public forecasts range from near-term dates to the view that the concept may require architectures or capabilities that current approaches do not provide. Such forecasts are opinions under substantial uncertainty, not reproducible measurements of time remaining.

**How is AGI different from an LLM?**
A large language model learns statistical regularities from pretraining and generates responses conditioned on context. Applications can augment it with tools, retrieval, memory and external execution. AGI would require substantially broader, reliable generalization across cognitive domains; neither fluent text generation nor tool use alone demonstrates that threshold.

**Would AGI necessarily be conscious?**
No. General task performance and subjective experience are distinct questions. Scientific and philosophical accounts of consciousness remain contested, and the technical definition of AGI does not require a particular answer to that debate.

**What do ANI, AGI and ASI mean?**
**ANI** (Artificial Narrow Intelligence) describes systems specialized for a relatively constrained range of tasks. **AGI** (Artificial General Intelligence) denotes a hypothetical system with broad human-comparable cognitive generality. **ASI** (Artificial Superintelligence) would exceed leading human performance across essentially all cognitive domains. These are conceptual distinctions, not standardized product labels.
