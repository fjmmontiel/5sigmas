Arrivals or concurrency

Under open-loop load, requests arrive at a fixed rate even when the server slows down.

Under closed-loop load, each client waits for a response before sending another request.

These are different experiments: the second can reduce arrivals precisely when latency increases.

Divide by successful tasks

A run costs twenty-four euros and makes one thousand attempts.

Nine hundred and twenty pass the success criterion fixed in advance.

Cost per attempt is €0.024; per successful task, about €0.0261. Failures also cost money.

Energy accumulates power

An illustrative constant six hundred watts over three hours consumes 1.8 kWh.

Dividing that energy by 920 successful tasks gives about 1.96 Wh per task.

This is not a measurement: power and outcomes must refer to the same window and system.

Draw what you measure

GPU telemetry does not necessarily include CPU, RAM, networking and system losses.

A wall meter observes a different boundary from the accelerator sensor.

Comparing them without a boundary mixes quantities. Rated power is not measured consumption either.

Queuing changes the regime

In this illustrative M/M/1 model, the server processes five requests per second on average.

As arrivals approach capacity, mean response time grows as one divided by five minus lambda.

At four arrivals per second, it is one second. A low-load test does not describe saturation.

Preserve the variation

Three equivalent repetitions take 1.0, 1.2 and 1.1 seconds in this example.

Publishing only the fastest removes information about variation between runs.

Fix warmup, cache state and protocol. Compare distributions and retain errors, not just the best scalar.
