Price can hide a quantity change

Ten services at ten euros each mean one hundred euros of spending.

Twenty services of equal quality at five euros each also mean one hundred.

At first-period prices, volume rises from one hundred to two hundred: distinguish nominal and real GDP.

A service and its measurement are not identical

Cooking at home for one’s own consumption provides a service without a market transaction.

Buying the equivalent service for fifty euros adds market production.

That accounting boundary does not prove the earlier service had no value; household accounts complement GDP.

The total can rise while the per-person figure falls

Output rises to one hundred twenty while population reaches fifteen.

Gross and net domestic product differ

GDP of 100 minus 20 of fixed-capital consumption leaves net domestic product of 80.

In another period, GDP of 105 minus 30 leaves net domestic product of 75.

GDP is gross: it does not deduct fixed-capital consumption.

Time can change without more output

The same one hundred units are produced in an eight-hour workday.

An improvement preserves quantity and quality with six hours.

Spending is not all the value received
