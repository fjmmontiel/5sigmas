# From quartz to a silicon disk

[From Mine to Cluster](00_presentacion_serie.md) · [Atlas](atlas.md)

A **wafer** is a thin disk of silicon used to make many chips at once. Each chip is a tiny electronic circuit. The silicon in that disk comes from quartz, which is made of silica, a compound of silicon and oxygen.

## From many small crystals to a single crystal

The first production step separates silicon from oxygen. The resulting silicon is purified further until it meets chipmaking limits on impurities. That material is called **electronic-grade polysilicon**. It is pure enough for chipmaking and contains many small crystals.

A crystal is an orderly arrangement of atoms. To give the whole wafer a uniform structure, the plant melts the polysilicon and grows one cylindrical crystal. It slices the crystal into wafers, then polishes and inspects them.

The vessel that holds the molten silicon is called a **crucible**. The crucible is made by melting high-purity quartz and shaping it into a vessel. Its purity limits how many impurities can enter the molten silicon.
